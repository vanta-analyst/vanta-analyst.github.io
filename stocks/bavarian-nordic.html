      Revenue is expected at about DKK 5.7bn in 2026 – below 7.1bn in 2023 and 6.2bn in 2025 – because mpox sales (Public Preparedness) fall to DKK 2.5bn. Travel Health grows 26%, but EPS is expected −27% in 2026.

      The pipeline behind the current vaccines is early: Lyme disease is preclinical, EBV enters phase 1 at the end of 2026, and equine encephalitis is in phase 2 awaiting defence funding. Growth after 2027 rests on existing travel vaccines.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −10 of −35
    • Customer concentration
      Public Preparedness (mpox/Jynneos) was 42% of revenue in H1 and is sold to a few government customers (the US and EU partners). Orders are lumpy, and the segment is expected to normalise towards about DKK 2bn a year.
      investing.com
      −4
    • Regulation (US)
      Vimkunya guidance has been cut from DKK 250m to 200m because the CDC has not yet adopted the ACIP recommendation from April 2025; sales were only DKK 78m in H1. US vaccine policy has become a real risk.
      idse.net
      −3
    • Management change
      Paul Chaplin, who has led the company for about 20 years, is stepping down; new CEO Tarja Stenvall (from Sanofi) only starts on 15 October 2026 after a long search.
      fiercepharma.com
      −2
    • Inventory and return risk
      TBE vaccines with a short shelf life (now 24 months) create a risk of returns in the autumn, and management itself says the 61% Q2 gross margin may not be structural.
      investing.com
      −1
