    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 3 findings    −8 of −35
    • Concentration in one small economy
      The bank has about 80% market share in Greenland, an economy of about 56,000 people that depends on fishing and the block grant. There is no geographical diversification, and net interest income fell 14% y/y in Q3 2025.
      ugebrev.dk
      −3
    • Speculative share price and liquidity
      DR calls the rise 'striking': the share price is driven by speculation about US investment after Trump's statements, not by fundamentals. On average only about 2,400 shares are traded a day (about DKK 2.9m).
      dr.dk
      −3
    • Dividend tax for Danish investors
      The bank withholds dividend tax at Sermersooq's total tax rate (about 42%), and Greenland has no general double-taxation agreement. That makes the high dividend yield worth less to a Danish investor.
      banken.gl
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Bank: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful (stockanalysis shows P/FCF 2.2 and an FCF margin of 221%, caused by deposit movements); valued on P/B and P/E, and the dividend is measured against net income. No analysts cover the stock (forecast = 404), and it trades very little. The 6.6% effective tax rate in stockanalysis is not explained. Insider trading, the shareholder base (over 2,500 shareholders, mostly local) and rating have not been examined. The exact refund entitlement for Danish investors' Greenlandic dividend tax is unclear in the sources (the bank's site: no general double taxation agreement).
