    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −14 of −35
    • Macro and peace risk
      77% of the order book is in Defence Systems and Missiles & Aerostructures, and the case rests on NATO rearmament and support for Ukraine (NASAMS). A ceasefire or budget pressure would hit both volume and the multiple. The stock is −4.6% in a year and below its 200-day average.
      tipranks.com
      −4
    • Customer concentration
      Customers are almost exclusively states: the Norwegian armed forces, the US (JSM, NSM framework with the US Marine Corps) and NASAMS countries such as Kuwait. The states decide orders, timing and prepayments; the share per customer is not disclosed.
      ad-hoc-news.de
      −3
    • Supply chain and execution
      Management itself calls security of supply the main risk – not demand – in the middle of a capacity build-out across several plants. Subcontractor Kitron reports allocation of memory chips, processors and circuit boards into H2.
      investing.com
      −3
    • Acquisition risk (Zone 5)
      90% of the US missile start-up Zone 5 was bought in June 2026 at an undisclosed price. Management talks about over NOK 10bn of annual revenue in the medium term from missiles that are not yet in series production – an unproven bet with integration risk.
      thedefensepost.com
      −2
    • Governance and state ownership
      The Norwegian state has kept the majority in both Kongsberg Gruppen and Kongsberg Maritime; the free float is only 411m of 880m shares. Minority shareholders have limited influence, and export policy is set politically.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Kongsberg Maritime was spun off on 22/4/2026 (1 KMAR share per KOG share) and trades separately as KMAR. What remains is a pure defence and technology company (Defence Systems, Missiles & Aerostructures, Discovery). stockanalysis' 2024–25 figures in the forecast are restated for continuing operations, but the 2021–23 ratio history and TTM net income (NOK 7.34bn) include Maritime until April, so trailing P/E 52, ROE and a net margin of 20.5% are distorted. Equity fell to NOK 13.1bn at the spin-off, so P/B 21 cannot be compared with history. The clean measure is a forward P/E of about 41 on 2026E EPS of 7.38 (EPS falls 18% due to the spin-off). Historical prices are adjusted for the 1:5 split (June 2025) and the spin-off. Net cash rests on customer prepayments (negative working capital −NOK 5.2bn). Insider trades have not been examined thoroughly.
