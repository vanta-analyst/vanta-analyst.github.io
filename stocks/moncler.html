    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 3 findings    −9 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue has stalled: €3,109m in 2024, 3,132 in 2025 (+0.75%) and 3,196 TTM (+2%); EPS is €2.35 TTM vs €2.24 in 2022 – four years without real earnings growth per share. The Moncler brand grew only 3% in Q2 2026 at constant exchange rates.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • ROIC trend
      ROIC has fallen from 23.2% (2022) through 21.2% and 20.8% to 17.5% TTM, because the store network and capital are growing faster than earnings; still well above the WACC of 9.5%.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Product breadth and the segment of the future
      The business rests on down jackets and the winter season; management itself points to the shift to 'buy now, wear now' and weaker spring/summer collections as a structural question, and H2 is 'the most important period of the year'.
      investing.com
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Luxury clothing does not fit the industry list (placed under Consumer: food, beverages & restaurants as the nearest consumer sector). Stockanalysis' net debt (-€93m) includes about €1.2bn of lease debt; excluding leases the company has net cash of €1.11bn at 30/6. FCF after leases (€556m TTM) is used instead of FCF (€799m). No full-year guidance; H2 (autumn/winter) is the decisive season, and Q3 is due 26/10. Several price targets were cut in September (including Morgan Stanley 57 to €50), but the median is still about €59 – not a finding. Peers (Prada, Burberry, Hermès) have not been compared systematically.
