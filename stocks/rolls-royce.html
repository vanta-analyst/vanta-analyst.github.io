    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 2 findings    −5 of −30
    • Growth quality (one-off lift)
      Contractual margin improvements in Civil Aerospace contributed £497m in H1 2026, equal to about 20% of underlying operating profit of £2,534m. Management itself expects a smaller contribution in H2. Part of the margin lift is thus renegotiation and catch-ups, not recurring operations.
      rolls-royce.com
      −3
    • Future segment
      Rolls-Royce is present only in widebody engines and has no product in the narrowbody market, which is the largest by volume. The comeback with UltraFan 30 (a programme of about GBP 3bn) still lacks a partner and an aircraft maker; test running is not expected until 2028.
      leehamnews.com
      −2

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −8 of −35
    • Cash flow and customer prepayments
      The cash rests on customer prepayments. Contract liabilities (unearned revenue) are £17.4bn vs net cash of £2.1bn. Growth in the netted LTSA balance fell in H1 to £86m from £472m a year earlier, so this tailwind to FCF is fading.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Macro (Middle East and flight hours)
      Rolls-Royce now expects flight hours for large engines at the low end of 115–120% of the 2019 level. The Gulf airlines are large Trent customers, and GE Aerospace cut its flight hours forecast in the spring because of the conflict. The aftermarket is the largest source of profit.
      rolls-royce.com
      −2
    • Supply chain
      Supply chain problems in aviation cost GBP 150–200m in cash flow in 2026, and the outflow continues to a lesser extent in 2027.
      rolls-royce.com
      −2
    • Durability (Trent 1000/XWB)
      The durability programmes are still running. Only just under half of the Trent 1000 TEN fleet has received new HPT blades, and life extensions on the Trent XWB-84 are not expected to be certified until the turn of the year. Historically this has led to AOG costs and compensation to customers.
      rolls-royce.com

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The share price is in pence (GBX), while the financial figures are in pounds. Own multiple history 2021–23 stems from the crisis and turnaround years and is therefore a skewed basis for comparison, so the finding rests mostly on EV/EBITDA, P/FCF and peers (Safran, MTU, GE Aerospace). Reported profit differs greatly from underlying: statutory PBT in H1 was £1,931m vs £2,495m underlying, and 2025 was lifted by recognition of deferred tax. The offsetting item has not been reviewed in detail. Net cash of £2.1bn is financed by customer prepayments (LTSA) and therefore does not count as a plus. Pension, SMR consumption and tariffs are not examined in depth. No discretionary insider purchases, only tax sales on vesting.
