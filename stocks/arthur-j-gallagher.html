    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 5 findings    −15 of −30
    • ROIC
      ROIC has fallen from 10.5% (2021) to 8.6%, 7.4%, 9.3% and 7.2% (2025) and 6.6% TTM, just above a WACC of 6.4%. The acquisitions (most recently AssuredPartners for about $14bn) fill the balance sheet with goodwill; Aon, by comparison, is at 16%.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue has almost doubled since 2021 ($7.97bn to 15.1bn, +31% TTM), but growth is mainly bought: organic growth in Brokerage was 5% in Q2 (base commissions and fees only 4%). The share count has risen about 25% since 2021 (+6.6% in a year), and GAAP EPS has only gone from $4.37 to 6.03 and has fallen from $6.50 in 2024.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Quality of earnings
      GAAP EPS in Q2 fell 11% to $1.25, while adjusted EPS rose 23% to $2.84. The gap is amortisation of customer lists and recurring one-offs: $113m of integration costs, $40m for severance and leases and $70m of acquisition-related adjustments in Q2 alone in Brokerage.
      sec.gov
      −3
    • Margin
      The adjusted EBITDAC margin in Brokerage fell from 36.1% to 33.3% in Q2, mainly because Q2 2025 included about $144m of interest income on the AssuredPartners proceeds, but also because of higher IT costs. The net margin has fallen from 13.4% (2024) to 10.4% TTM.
      sec.gov
      −1
    • Capital allocation
      An $8.5bn share issue and new debt funded AssuredPartners, and management now has capacity for about $10bn of further acquisitions over two years (30 signed term sheets with about $400m of revenue), while ROIC falls.
