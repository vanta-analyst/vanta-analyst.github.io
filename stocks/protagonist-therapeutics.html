    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −15 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue is one-off payments, not sales: USD 434m in 2024, 46m in 2025, expected 542m in 2026 and 203m in 2027 (-62%) with EPS 4.79 → -0.20. Q2 2026 (213.5m) was 192.4m from Takeda's opt-out payment and no royalties.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Margin and core operations
      Without milestones, operations are loss-making: R&D 42.1 and administration USD 12.6m in Q2 (approx. 220m a year), and management expects R&D to rise markedly in H2 (PN-881 phase 2, obesity programmes). Royalties do not yet cover the costs.
      1stoncology.com
      −4
    • Moat and control
      Protagonist controls none of its approved products: Icotyde is commercially owned by J&J (royalty only 6–10%, approx. 7.25% on average at 4bn USD sales), and the company sold its 50/50 profit share in rusfertide to Takeda for 400m USD plus 14–29% royalty. The obesity candidate PN-477 is only in phase 1 (oral version delayed to 2027) in a crowded field.
      1stoncology.com
      −4
    • Capital allocation
      The share count rises about 4% a year (share-based pay and options), and there are no buybacks despite net cash of USD 840m; costs are being stepped up in new early programs.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Royalty/milestone biotech: revenue and earnings are driven by one-off payments (Takeda opt-out USD 200m recognised in Q2 2026; a further USD 275m triggered by the rusfertide approval on 28/8/2026 is included in Q3), so trailing P/E, ROIC and FCF are not representative. J&J does not disclose Icotyde sales, so the royalty income is unknown. The takeover chatter (WSJ, October 2025) has been neither confirmed nor denied. Peers (royalty companies) not systematically compared. The reel claim of '10x in 2026' requires about USD 93bn in market cap – unrealistic; even a takeover typically gives a 30–60% premium.
