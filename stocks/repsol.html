    • Quality of growth
      Revenue is largely flat (€44.5bn in 2021 vs €48.3bn in 2025); the 2026 lift comes from refining margins (Industrial 1,243 of 1,838m adjusted earnings in Q2) and an inventory gain of €823m. Analysts expect EPS +106% in 2026 and −25% in 2027.
      infobae.com
      −3
    • Moat and pricing power
      Repsol is a price taker on both crude oil and refining margins; the gross margin has been 26–28% in 2021–2025, and only the peaks in oil and product prices lift it (31.6% TTM).
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Future segment
      Low Carbon Generation delivered only €10m in adjusted earnings in Q2 2026, and Repsol is selling 49.99% of a 705 MW portfolio to Masdar for about €150m – the transition segment is not growing into a core earnings driver.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Adjusted net result H1 2026 was €2,711m vs reported €2,201m; reported earnings also include a positive inventory effect of €823m from higher oil prices, which is not repeatable. Stockanalysis' fall in share count (−4.5%) matches the share capital (1,157 → 1,105m shares in 2025) and the cancellation of 15.8m shares in July 2026; 'Issuance of Common Stock' (€753m TTM) is the sale of minority stakes, not share issuance. FCF after leasing (about €0.5bn a year) is about €1.7bn TTM and still covers dividend + buyback of €1.38bn. The price (€29.80) is above the average price target (€28.51) and the median (€26.50); several targets were raised in September, others are stale after the +90% price rise – not used as a finding. Spanish energy tax, Libya/Algeria and insider trading have not been examined. Credit rating has not been examined.
