Business

Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 5 findings−11 of −30
  • ROIC and margin
    ROIC 10.3% TTM and only 8.1% in 2025 vs 14.3–16.1% in 2022–2023; the operating margin was 10.8% in 2025 and has only been lifted to 13.6% TTM by an oil price around 100 USD. The return swings with the commodity price and has not become stronger.
    stockanalysis.com
    −2
  • Quality of growth
    Revenue fell three years in a row (USD 263bn in 2022 to 182bn in 2025), and the lift to 196bn TTM and expected +17% in 2026 comes from price, not volume: about 15% of oil and gas production (Qatar, Iraq, UAE offshore) is shut down due to the conflict, and analysts expect EPS −8% in 2027.
    stockanalysis.com
    −3
  • Moat and pricing power
    As an oil and gas producer, TotalEnergies is a price taker; refining and marketing (over 90% of revenue) are low-margin. The integrated LNG business gives some advantage, but no real pricing power.
    stockanalysis.com
    −2
  • Future segment
    Integrated Power is to grow over 20% a year to 100–120 TWh in 2030, but the segment's revenue has fallen from USD 27.3bn (2022–2023) to 18.9bn TTM, and the power business has far lower returns than oil and gas.
    euronews.com
    −2
  • Capital allocation
    In 2025 the company distributed USD 17.3bn (dividend 8.4 + buyback 8.9) vs FCF of 11.6bn; the difference was borrowed, and net debt rose from USD 17.1bn (2023) to 32.2bn (2025).
