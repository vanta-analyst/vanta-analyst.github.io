    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 5 findings    −13 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Analysts expect EPS +115% and revenue +22% in 2026, but that is due to a realised liquids price of USD 97.9/barrel and European gas at USD 15.8/mmbtu in Q2. Production grew only 3%. That is the commodity cycle, not a stronger company.
      equinor.com
      −5
    • ROIC through the cycle
      ROIC 16.5% (TTM), but only 10.3% in 2025 vs 17.6% in 2024, 27% in 2023 and 67% in 2022. Returns follow oil and gas prices.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Future segment
      Power (renewables), which is to carry the transition, accounts for only about USD 2.4bn of 114bn of revenue (TTM). The rest is oil and gas, where long-term demand is uncertain.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Moat
      Equinor is a price taker on oil and gas. The advantage is low costs: NCS projects with breakevens below USD 35/barrel and payback in under 2.5 years.
      equinor.com
      −2
    • Capital allocation
      In 2025 USD 10.7bn was paid out (buybacks 5.9 + dividends 4.8) vs FCF of only USD 6.0bn. Net cash of USD 13.3bn in 2022 has turned into net debt of USD 11.9bn in 2025.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Accounts in USD, price in NOK. The net margin (8%) is low due to Norwegian petroleum tax (effective tax 73%), not weak operations – the operating margin is 29%. 2026 earnings rest on an oil price of about USD 98/barrel and European gas at USD 15.8/mmbtu (Q2). A peer check against Aker BP (EV/EBITDA 2.8, P/B 1.9, ROIC 8.8%) does not show Equinor to be relatively expensive – hence no peer deduction. The Ørsted stake (about 10%) has not been valued.
