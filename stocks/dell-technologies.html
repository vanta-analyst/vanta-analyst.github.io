    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 2 findings    −4 of −30
    • Moat and margin
      The gross margin has fallen from 23.9% (FY24) to 19.9% TTM because low-margin AI servers take up more. Servers are a competitive market (Supermicro, HPE, ODMs). The operating margin has still risen from 4.7% (FY22) to 9.9% TTM and was 12.6% non-GAAP in Q2, and ROIC has risen from 10.7% to 29.4%.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      Growth (+58% in Q2) comes from AI servers (+100%) and traditional servers (+122%) on top of an AI capex cycle and rising memory prices. Revenue fell 14% in FY24, which shows how cyclical hardware is.
      sec.gov
      −1

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 6 findings    −22 of −35
    • Cash flow and working capital
      TTM, inventory rose $14.3bn and receivables $7.9bn, while payables rose $22.3bn. FCF of $8.6bn therefore rests on paying suppliers later. In Q2 operating cash flow was only $2.2bn on $47bn of revenue.
      stockanalysis.com
      −8
    • Customer concentration
      The $95bn AI backlog (+$43.7bn in one quarter) sits with a few large AI customers (including xAI and CoreWeave); the share of the largest customers is not disclosed.
      investing.com
      −3
    • Debt
      Net debt $23.6bn (debt $35.3bn vs cash $11.6bn), current ratio 0.96 and Altman Z 2.26. Net debt/EBITDA has fallen from 2.4 (FY23) to 1.3, though.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Insider trading
      Silver Lake entities sold continuously in June, July and 3–11/9 (including $44.7m on 3/9 and $23.2m on 11/9 from one fund alone), and board member Egon Durban sold $19.5m on 4/9 plus smaller amounts. New sales were filed on 14/9 and 17/9. No insider buying found.
      stocktitan.net
      −4
    • Governance
      An ERISA class action from January 2026 is mentioned in the 10-Q; Michael Dell controls the votes through share classes.
      sec.gov
      −1
    • Macro
      Rising memory prices push up component costs, and Gartner expects fewer PCs sold in 2026. PC sales (+20% in Q2) may have been pulled forward ahead of price increases. At the same time Dell depends on AI capex holding up.
