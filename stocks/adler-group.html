    Bull case

    Berlin housing prices rise 20–30%, and interest rates fall, so the portfolio (EUR 3.5bn) rises to 4.2–4.5bn. Even then the debt (about 3.45bn) and the perpetual bonds (about 2.3bn) must be covered first; only if the perpetuals can be redeemed at a low price or need not be paid in full is there value for the shares. A 3–10x from EUR 15m market cap is mathematically easy, but requires an unlikely outcome.

    Bear case

    Property values are standing still, and interest expenses continue to eat equity; the 2028–2030 maturity wall requires a third restructuring where debt is converted to shares and the old shares are effectively wiped out (−95–100%).
