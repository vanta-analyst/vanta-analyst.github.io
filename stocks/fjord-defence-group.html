    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −19 of −30
    • Margin
      As reported, the group is still loss-making: EBIT −134m NOK and net result −138m TTM, weighed down by the old seismic business, amortisation and acquisition costs. The defence segment showed a pro forma EBITDA margin of 17.3% in Q2 (42.4m on 245.4m), but that is pro forma and before amortisation of excess values.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue about tripled in Q2, but almost all growth was bought: Fjord Defence AS (2025), Fjord Defence Marine (June 2026, EV about 170m) and PartnerTech Karlskoga (August 2026, EV about SEK 900m). Organic growth is not disclosed, and the 2029 ambition (EBITDA 400-500m) presupposes more acquisitions.
      mfn.se
      −6
    • Capital allocation
      The share count has risen from about 18m (2024) to about 117m (+550%), and shares worth about NOK 926m have been issued in 1.5 years. The acquisitions were done at sensible multiples (about 5x EBITDA for Marine and about 8x for PartnerTech), but paid with shares at NOK 15–16.50 and debt. Goodwill was 550m even before PartnerTech.
      stockanalysis.com
      −6
    • Moat
      PartnerTech has over 30 years of sole-source relationships with two of Europe's most important defence OEMs, which is a real position, but it is contract manufacturing to the customer's design with limited pricing power. Fjord Defence AS won its first series contract with a new customer in September (NOK 11.5m fixed).
      mfn.se
      −2

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −15 of −35
    • Customer concentration
      About 92% of PartnerTech's revenue (which will become about 60–65% of the group) comes from defence OEMs, and the bulk from two customers. Loss or insourcing at one of them would hit the group hard.
      mfn.se
      −5
    • Integration and execution risk
      Three acquisitions in approx. a year, of which PartnerTech (EV SEK 900m) is larger than the rest of the group combined. A small holding company must integrate Swedish and Norwegian units while winding down the old seismic business. Guidance for 2027 only comes with the Q3 report.
      mfn.se
      −5
    • Debt
      The cash part of the PartnerTech purchase price (SEK 451m) was financed with the issue and a draw on the acquisition facility at Nordea. Interest-bearing debt was NOK 236m already at 30/6, before PartnerTech and its own net debt. Estimated net debt is about 2x pro forma EBITDA.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Legacy of seismic and dilution
      The company is the former Axxis Geo Solutions/Aquila Holdings with a multi-client seismic library (Norway and Egypt), which has produced large losses (EBIT −110m in 2024). Shareholders have been diluted about 6 times in 1.5 years, and the 2029 ambition probably requires more issues.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    No analysts at stockanalysis (forecast 404). Stockanalysis' share count (100.05m) and market cap (NOK 1.38bn) are outdated: after the directed issue (25m shares at NOK 16.50) there were 101.12m shares, and on completion of the PartnerTech acquisition on 26/8, 15.77m consideration shares were added. That gives c. 116.9m shares × NOK 13.70 = NOK 1.60bn ≈ DKK 1,101m (Nordnet: DKK 1,115m). Stockanalysis' TTM figures (revenue 117m, EBIT −134m) cover only a small part of the current group and include the old seismic business (formerly Axxis Geo Solutions/Aquila Holdings). Pro forma net debt after PartnerTech (EV SEK 900m, partly financed with a Nordea acquisition facility) is not disclosed; c. NOK 400m is an estimate. Operating company with no real 'burn' in the defence segment (pro forma EBITDA guidance 190–230m for 2026), so runway is set to null. Short data not found.
