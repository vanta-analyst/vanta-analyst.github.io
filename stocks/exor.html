    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    A holding company: stockanalysis' revenue (−4.0bn), net profit (−4.3bn), ROE/ROIC and P/E are fair-value changes on the portfolio (especially Stellantis) and not meaningful; the PEG formula will therefore give 'n/a'. Valuation is on net asset value (NAV EUR 157.9 per share at 30/6/2026) and P/B (0.45, equal to NAV). The subsidiaries' own findings (Ferrari, Stellantis, Philips, CNH) are not counted again – only Exor's exposure, losses and possible capital needs. Stellantis is scored separately. Share count −3.9% in a year matches buybacks of about EUR 1bn in 2025 (funded by the sale of 4% of Ferrari in Feb 2025, i.e. a core asset) and therefore earns no plus. The new EUR 500m buyback (about a 54% discount to NAV, funded by cash from the sales of GEDI, Lifenet and NUO) is accretive to NAV per share, but together with the dividend exceeds the holding's FCF (444m) and therefore earns no plus. The holding's own FCF (444m) is dividends from subsidiaries minus costs. Exor's historical average discount was not found in a source; history is assessed on P/B 2022–2025. Unlisted assets (Lingotto, Institut Mérieux, Louboutin, The Economist) are valued by Exor itself. Insider trading in 2026 has not been examined.
