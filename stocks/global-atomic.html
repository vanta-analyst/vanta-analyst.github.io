    Bull case

    The DFC conditions are met in 2027, a JV partner pays for a minority stake instead of new issues, and Dasa (68m lb over 23+ years, Africa's highest-grade uranium resource) comes into operation in 2028 with a high uranium price. With 80% of a project in which US$228m has already been invested, a market cap of C$0.6–1bn (3–4x today's about C$227m) could be possible – but only if the dilution is kept down.

    Bear case

    The junta changes the terms (as with Orano), the export route stays closed, or the DFC conditions cannot be met; then the remaining approx. C$215m of equity must be raised at ever lower prices, or the project stalls with US$228m sunk in Niger. Shareholders could be diluted by a further 50–100%, and in a nationalization the stock is close to worthless.
