    • ROIC through the cycle

    • Margin
      Net margin 9.3% (TTM) vs 0.1% in 2024, 0.3% in 2023 and 2.7% in 2021. The operating margin swings between 3% and 16% with gas and fertiliser prices.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      EBITDA before special items rose 39% to USD 906m in Q2, while external deliveries fell 17% (Africa & Asia −28%) and downtime cost USD 120m. The whole improvement is price margin, not volume.
      investing.com
      −5
    • Moat
      A strong distribution network and premium products (nitrates, NPK), but European production is more expensive than US and Middle Eastern gas-based ammonia. Yara bought Gulf Coast Ammonia precisely to reduce its dependence on European energy prices.
      yara.com
      −3
    • Capital allocation
      The dividend swings wildly: USD 1.32bn paid in 2023 (above FCF of 1.15bn), then only 0.12–0.13bn in 2024–2025. Now an ammonia plant is being bought for USD 1.3bn while ammonia prices are high.
      stockanalysis.com
      −1

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 3 findings    −12 of −35
    • Temporary margins
      The blockade of the Strait of Hormuz caused a supply shock and swings in urea prices, which lift Yara's margins. The price uncertainty also led to a slow start to the season in the northern hemisphere. Analysts expect EPS −21% and revenue −3.6% in 2027.
      yara.com
      −5
    • Gas supply
      Yara had to cut ammonia and urea production at Babrala (India) when the war interrupted gas supply from the Middle East. European production remains exposed to gas prices of about USD 15–16/mmbtu.
      bloomberg.com
      −4
    • Acquisition risk and debt
      Gulf Coast Ammonia (1.3m t/year in Texas City) costs USD 1.3bn, awaits approval and is only to run steadily by the end of 2026. It comes on top of net debt of USD 3.1bn.
      investing.com
      −3
