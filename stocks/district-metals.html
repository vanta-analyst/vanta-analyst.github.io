    Bull case

    The new government leaves the uranium reform in place (the Social Democrats accept uranium as a by-product, and uranium is precisely a minor part of Viken's revenue), a European energy or utility partner comes in, and the market starts to price the project at 5–10% of the PEA NPV (about USD 200–400m). That equates to 3–6x from today.

    Bear case

    The red-green government reintroduces a ban or veto, or the alum shale inquiry ends with strict requirements. Viken is then effectively unusable, and the stock could fall to cash value (about C$0.09 per share) – a fall of about 80%.

    Penny analysis (Under DKK 3). Exploration/development company with uranium and polymetallic projects in alum shale in Sweden (main asset Viken in Jämtland, 100% owned via Bergslagen Metals AB), with no revenue. Fiscal year July–June; latest figures at 30/6-2026. The PEA (June 2026) is based only on indicated resource, but assumes a pug-roasting process that has not been tested at scale on Swedish alum shale, and large by-product revenues (vanadium, potassium etc.).
