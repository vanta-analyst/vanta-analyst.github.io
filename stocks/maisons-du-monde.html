    Bull case

    The new owners turn operations around: revenue stabilises around 850m, cost savings (15m in H1) lift EBIT to +5% (about 40m), and the company becomes FCF-positive. That could justify a value of EUR 300-400m (1.6–2x now). A 3-10x is unlikely, because 95% of the shares already sit with the funds at EUR 0.28, and a squeeze-out typically happens near that level.

    Bear case

    Revenue continues to fall 7-10% a year, cash is drained during 2027, and TIROX carries out a new capital increase or a buyout at a low price. With only 8 months of runway and a consortium that already owns 95%, the minority could lose 50-80%.
