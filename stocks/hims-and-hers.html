    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −21 of −30
    • Margin
      The gross margin has fallen from 82% (2023) and 76% (Q2 2025) to 64% in Q2 2026, as Novo products are resold at low margin. The operating margin was −12.8% in Q2 and the GAAP loss USD 86.3m vs a profit of 42.5m a year earlier; adjusted EBITDA fell from 82.2 to 60.3m USD.

      −7
    • Quality of growth
      Growth of 38% is largely bought: the US grew only 16%, while the rest of the world (USD 131m) grew 17 times after the acquisition of Australian Eucalyptus in June. Revenue per subscriber +21% is due to more expensive branded drugs with low margin – bigger, not stronger.
      investors.hims.com
      −4
    • Moat
      Growth in 2024–25 was built on copycat GLP-1 (compounded semaglutide). In March 2026 Hims stopped marketing them in the US and now sells Ozempic and Wegovy at the same self-pay price as other telehealth platforms – no price advantage, low switching costs and heavy dependence on marketing.
      investors.hims.com
      −5
    • Capital allocation
      From a net cash of 289 m USD (2024) to a net debt of 705 m: convertible bonds 1,365 m USD, 318 m cash used on acquisitions in H1 and 886 m in contingent/deferred purchase consideration and assumed liabilities. ROIC −10.5%, current ratio 0.93.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −17 of −35
    • Regulation and DOJ
      HHS's general counsel referred Hims to the Department of Justice (DOJ) in February 2026 for possible violations of the Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act (FDCA) after the plan to sell a copy of the Wegovy pill; the stock fell 16% in one day. The case is unresolved and may bring injunctions or fines; Hims provisioned USD 47.5m for litigation in Q2, and the FDA again warned against copy GLP-1s in September.
      axios.com
      −6
    • FTC lawsuit
      On 29/7-2026 the FTC along with California and Utah sued Hims for sharing sensitive health data with ad platforms (Meta, Snap) and for misleading subscriptions that are hard to cancel (ROSCA). Injunctions, refunds and fines are sought – directly against the business model.
      fiercehealthcare.com
      −3
    • Partner dependence (Novo Nordisk)
      Novo terminated the first collaboration in June 2025 after two months and accused Hims of selling 'copies', sued in February 2026 and entered a new agreement in March 2026. The weight-loss business now depends on a partner that has shown it can pull the plug.
      goodmorningamerica.com
      −4

      22.7% of the shares are sold short, beta 2.4, and the price is down 47% over 52 weeks; Altman Z-score 1.98 (gray zone).

      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Industry set to 'Health insurance & services', because Hims is a subscription and telehealth platform selling consultations and others' medicines – not a drug developer. Financials through Q2 2026; Q3 comes c. 2/11-2026. The GAAP figures are depressed by one-offs (litigation provision 47.5m, acquisition costs 28.8m, restructuring USD 38m in H1). The Eucalyptus purchase price is not disclosed directly. The outcome of the DOJ referral and the FTC case is unknown. Peers (Teladoc, LifeMD et al.) not systematically compared. Hims is not a peptide company; the claim of '10x in 2026' requires c. USD 72bn in market cap – unrealistic for a loss-making company with USD 1.4bn in convertible debt.
