  • Moat
    Salmon is a commodity, so the market sets the price. The licences (MAB 29,800 t in five West Fjords) are limited but taxed 3–4 times harder than in Norway, so they give no real price advantage.

    Q2 revenue rose from €12.6m to 32.5m purely on harvest volume (5,595 t vs 2,020 t), with low price achievement and harsh weather. There is no margin improvement per kg to show scale benefits.

    −3

  • Debt
    Net interest-bearing debt is €143m (excl. IFRS 16). Net debt/equity has risen from 0.35 (2021) to 1.79, and interest cover is −0.74x, so operations do not cover interest.
    stockanalysis.com
    −7
  • Dilution
    13.1m new shares in September 2025 (share count +41%, +27% a year on average), which shows an ongoing funding need.
    tipranks.com
    −5
  • Regulation and tax
    The Icelandic farming levy is ISK 41.66/kg in 2026, 3–4 times the Norwegian one. The new aquaculture law (presented in March 2026) is unresolved and points to stricter environmental and supervision requirements. Arctic Fish has therefore put new expansion investment on hold (21/8/2026).
    fishfarmingexpert.com
    −6
  • Biology and operations
    Mass mortality in 2022 (1,500–2,000 t) and an escape of about 3,500 salmon in Patreksfjörður in August 2023, which MAST asked to be officially investigated. The biological risk has repeated itself.
    en.wikipedia.org
    −4
  • Macro
    Rising feed prices, a strong NOK and lowered salmon price forecasts squeeze price achievement.
    salmonbusiness.com
    −5
  • Governance and liquidity
    Mowi owns the majority and has taken over sales (related party). The free float is only 17.6m shares with about 800 shares traded a day, so minority shareholders have little influence and an illiquid stock.
