    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 1 finding    −2 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Q2 volume (+5%) was lifted by the World Cup campaign, and concentrate sales rose only 4%. Analysts expect revenue +0.3% in 2027 and EPS +6.8%, so the strong 2026 looks like a peak. The margin (31.9% vs 28.7% in 2021) and ROIC (20.1% vs 16.2%) are still rising, though, so the business is stronger, not just bigger.

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 4 findings    −13 of −35
    • Lawsuits (IRS)
      The IRS transfer-pricing case: Coca-Cola owes about $6bn incl. interest for 2007–09 (deposited), and the same method applied to 2010–25 could cost about $14bn more. The case was argued orally in the 11th Circuit on 25/6/2026, where the judges seemed receptive to Coca-Cola. No ruling yet.
      news.bloombergtax.com
      −5
    • Insider trading
      Large, persistent insider selling with no purchases: chairman (and CEO until 31/3) James Quincey has sold about $119m in 2026 ($19.8m in March, 15.8m in May, 35.6m in June and 47.5m in July), CFO John Murphy $13.3m, EVP Jennifer Mann about $26m and the head of Latin America about $19m. Mostly option-related, but over $200m in total since March.
      secform4.com
      −4
    • Customers are shifting
      GLP-1 drugs and the US anti-sugar movement (MAHA) are a long-term threat to soft drinks; CEO Henrique Braun had to address the GLP-1 headwind at the AGM. Volume is still growing, though, and Coca-Cola Zero Sugar grew 13% in Q1.
      tikr.com
      −2
    • Macro and currency
      Currency contributed 4 percentage points of EPS growth in Q2 (comparable EPS +11%, i.e. about 7% ex currency) and 2 points of operating profit. Growth is driven by emerging markets, so a stronger dollar could quickly reverse the tailwind.
      investors.coca-colacompany.com
      −2

      64 consecutive years of dividend increases; payout ratio 64%. FCF guidance for 2026 is about $12.4bn vs dividends of about $9bn and only small buybacks. The FCF dip in 2024–25 ($4.7bn and 5.3bn) was due to one-off payments (the IRS deposit and the fairlife purchase price), not operations.
