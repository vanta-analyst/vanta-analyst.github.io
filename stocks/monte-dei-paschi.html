Cracks−12 of −35

Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers.

  • The price rests on Intesa's bid
    The price is tied to Intesa's €30.6bn bid, which depends on the Antitrust in-depth investigation, Consob's approval and Delfin's decision (17.5% of MPS). If the bid falls, the floor under the price disappears.
    forbes.it
    −4
  • Serial acquisitions
    Amid the Mediobanca integration (merger planned in Q4 2026), MPS is bidding EUR 34bn for Banco BPM (25.3bn, no premium) and Banca Generali (8.7bn), equal to about 2.8bn new shares at today's price (almost a doubling again). Requires a 2/3 majority and support from Crédit Agricole (29.3% of BPM, against) and Generali (50.2% of Banca Generali); BPM's board has unanimously rejected it.

Caveats

What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.

Bank: EV, ROIC and FCF are not meaningful; valuation on P/B, P/TBV and P/E, and the payout is held against net profit. The share price (EUR 11.96) reflects Intesa's exchange offer (1.6 Intesa shares + EUR 1 in cash per MPS share, today about EUR 11.80) and is above both the offer value and analysts' average price target (11.77); it is a bid play, not a fundamental valuation. Stockanalysis' trailing figures (EPS 1.11, P/E 10.8, ROE 15%) are distorted by the Mediobanca consolidation and the share count +111%; H1 2026 annualised gives about EUR 0.74 per share (P/E about 16). Forward P/E is compared only with peers because forward history is missing. A special dividend of EUR 4bn (Generali shares + EUR 1.208 in cash) is tied to the dual bid and not included. RoTE, cost/income, credit costs and DTA have not been investigated. Q3 results 5 Nov.; general meeting on the dual bid 29 Oct.
