    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −29 of −30
    • Margin
      Gross loss of 28.8m USD and operating loss of 189.4m USD TTM on revenue of 7.8m USD (operating margin -2,431%). The gross loss was 8.8m in Q2 alone, i.e. the robots lose money on every delivery before fixed costs. ROIC -41% is not counted again.
      stockanalysis.com
      −13
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue guidance for 2026 was cut from USD 26m to USD 9-10m after a drop in deliveries in Q2 and the loss of the expected Uber volume. Revenue grew only 9% from Q1 to Q2 despite a fleet of about 2,000 robots, and about half of food delivery revenue is advertising on the robots.
      marketbeat.com
      −7
    • Capital allocation
      The fleet was built well ahead of demand (capex USD 37m in 2025), and companies have been bought (Diligent for USD 25.7m and others), while the share count rose 44% in a year. Non-GAAP costs of USD 140–150m in 2026 compare with 9–10m in revenue.
      sec.gov
      −5
    • Moat
      Sidewalk delivery has many competitors (Starship, Coco, Avride, Nuro), and Serve depends on the platforms Uber Eats and DoorDash as customers and channel, so pricing power lies with the platforms.

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    Penny deep dive (the price is above DKK 3). Serve operates autonomous sidewalk robots for food delivery via Uber Eats and DoorDash and has bought Diligent Robotics (hospital robots, Jan. 2026). A price of USD 4.35 (9/10-2026) and 86.7m shares at 4/8-2026 (10-Q) give a market cap of USD 377m (DKK 2,483m); Nordnet showed DKK 2,723m, which may indicate further ATM sales after 4/8 (not verified). The latest report is Q2 2026 (30/6). The total size of the ATM agreement and remaining amount, and the warrants' exercise price, have not been found. The largest individual shareholders have not been examined. Analysts' price targets (average USD 12.14) are largely from before/around the guidance cut and are probably outdated.
