    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 5 findings    −17 of −30
    • Margin
      Operating margin only 5.0% and net margin 3.2% (TTM) – and that is a record period; in 2025 operating margin was 1.6% and in 2024 3.4%. Gross margin 16%. Even in a good year there is very little room for fuel and wage increases.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • ROIC
      ROIC 11.9% TTM, but only 2.7% in 2025 and 5.1% in 2024; ROCE 6.8%. The return on capital is low and swings sharply with the cycle.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Moat
      Finnair's historical advantage – the short route between Europe and Asia via Helsinki – is gone for as long as Russian airspace is closed. Middle East revenue has fallen from 203 m EUR (2024) to 63 m (TTM), and Asia revenue of 1.2 bn is still below the pre-2020 level.
      stockanalysis.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue grew only approx. 2% a year in 2023–2025 (2.99 → EUR 3.11bn). The 16% jump in Q2 2026 is compared with a quarter hit by strikes in 2025, so part of the growth is recovery, not structural progress.
      finnair.com
      −2
    • Capital allocation
      The capital raises after 2020 and capital loans from the state diluted shareholders massively (dilution of 604% in 2023 and 106% in 2024 according to stockanalysis), followed by a 1:100 reverse split in 2024.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −15 of −35
    • Fuel and the Middle East war
      2026 guidance assumes no significant disruptions in fuel supply; the prolonged war in the Middle East is an explicit risk. 76% of H2 fuel is hedged, and a 10% move in fuel price equals about EUR 18m.
      finnair.com
      −4
    • Debt and balance sheet
      Debt EUR 1.75bn against equity of only EUR 810m (debt/equity 2.2), interest cover 2.2x and Altman Z-score 0.86. Cash is seasonally high because of prepaid tickets.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Labour disputes
      H1 2025 was marked by months of strikes among pilots and other employees, which caused a loss. There is labour peace now, but the collective agreements have raised costs, and the risk of new conflicts is real in a strongly unionised company.
      pasazer.com
      −2
    • State ownership
      The Finnish state owns about 56%, and the free float is only about 90m of 205m shares. Political considerations (routes, jobs) may weigh heavier than minority shareholders' interests.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Fleet renewal
      The narrowbody fleet must be renewed. So far there are only letters of intent for 6 used A320ceo aircraft for delivery in 2027. An actual renewal requires large investments, which the thin margin and the balance sheet must carry.
      finnair.com
      −3
