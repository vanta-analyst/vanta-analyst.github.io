    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −10 of −30
    • Margin
      GAAP operating margin 15.8% (TTM) and adjusted about 27% in Q2; the data centre segment earns 31% (2.1 of 6.7bn $). Nvidia is at about 65%. Even without Xilinx amortisation ($544m in Q2) the margin is below half of Nvidia's.
      ir.amd.com
      −4
    • ROIC
      ROIC 9.8% (TTM), up from 0.8% in 2023, but below the stated cost of capital of 17.7%. The figure is depressed by $25.5bn of goodwill from Xilinx, so the underlying rate of return is higher.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Quality of earnings
      Investment gains of $483m make up approx. 21% of Q2 net income ($2.3bn). Share-based compensation is $1.9bn TTM (4.6% of revenue) and growing 16% a year. FCF in Q2 fell to $1.56bn as capex doubled to $808m.
      stockanalysis.com
      −2
    • Moat and pricing power
      AMD is number two behind Nvidia (CUDA). The gross margin of 56% vs Nvidia's about 75% shows weaker pricing power, and the big GPU deals were bought with equity warrants that, according to SemiAnalysis, can lower customers' real GPU price markedly.
      kucoin.com
      −2

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 7 findings    −28 of −35
    • Dilution
      OpenAI and Meta each hold warrants for up to 160m shares at $0.01 (in total approx. 20% of 1.63bn shares), which vest on GPU purchase milestones and price targets; OpenAI's last tranche requires $600, which the price is now above. None had vested as of 27/6. The share count also rises 1.1% a year despite buybacks.
      sec.gov
      −8
    • Customer concentration
      AI growth from 2H26 rests on a few customers: OpenAI (6 GW), Meta (up to 6 GW) and Anthropic (up to 2 GW) in Helios racks. OpenAI's ability to finance its commitments is an open question, and AMD does not disclose customer shares.
      247wallst.com
      −4
    • Customer financing and guarantees
      AMD has guaranteed up to $4.1bn of a commercial partner's data centre lease agreements and has entered data centre lease agreements for $4.5bn plus a further $9.5bn after the quarter, with terms of up to 16 years.
      sec.gov
      −3
    • Competition
      Nvidia dominates with Rubin and CUDA, and the hyperscalers are rolling out their own chips at gigawatt scale (TPU, Trainium, Maia, MTIA), often designed with Broadcom. AMD fights for second place against the in-house chips.
      spheron.network
      −4
    • Execution, inventory and obligations
      Helios (first rack-scale system, MI450) only begins to be delivered in Q3, and growth is back-loaded. Purchase commitments are $30.3bn ($17.4bn for the rest of 2026), and inventory has risen to $8.5bn (inventory turnover 2.4).
      sec.gov
      −3
    • Insider trading
      Lisa Su sold 2 × 125,000 shares (about $113m) in May and again in August and September; CTO Papermaster, CFO Jean Hu and Forrest Norrod have also sold in August-September. No insider buying, and insiders own only 0.44%.
      secform4.com
      −4
    • Export controls / geopolitics
      MI308 sales to China require a licence and surrendering 15% of China revenue to the US government and have shrunk to approx. $100m in Q1. All advanced production is at TSMC in Taiwan.
      trendforce.com
      −2

    Caveats

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    GAAP figures are depressed by about $2.2bn of annual amortisation from the Xilinx purchase (goodwill $25.5bn) and lifted by investment gains ($483m in Q2). Warrants to OpenAI and Meta for up to 160m shares each (about 20% of share capital) at $0.01 have not yet been earned and are not in the accounts. Client and Gaming (about 1/3 of revenue) are cyclical, but data centre dominates, so cyclical = no. No customer over 10% is disclosed in the 10-Q.
