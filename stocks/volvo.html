    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −12 of −30
    • Margin
      The operating margin is 10.4% TTM vs 14.6% in 2023 and 13.0% in 2024, and the gross margin has fallen to 24.4% from 26.4%. The adjusted Q2 margin of 11.7% is better, but still clearly below the peak.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • ROIC
      ROIC has fallen from 19.1% (2023) and 14.5% (2024) to 9.4% TTM; ROE from 28.8% to 20.9%. Volvo's own ROCE for the industrial operations is 26.8%, but the trend is the same: lower returns than two years ago.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Quality of growth
      Neither bigger nor stronger: revenue fell 4.6% in 2024 and 9.0% in 2025 and is SEK 471.5bn TTM vs 552bn in 2023; EPS is 17.62 vs 24.78 in 2024. Construction equipment has fallen from SEK 105bn to 78bn since 2023.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
    • Future segment (electric trucks)
      Volvo's share of the EU29 market for battery-electric heavy trucks has fallen to 16.3% from 33.8%, while its overall share is 19.6%. Deliveries of electric vehicles fell 2% (12 months), so competitors are catching up in the segment that has to carry the 2030 requirements.
      investing.com
      −3

    What the score does not capture, or where the data is weak.
    The B share (VOLV.B). Industry set to 'Machinery & automation' rather than 'Autos & parts': under GICS Volvo is a machinery and heavy-vehicle maker (trucks 68%, construction equipment 16%, buses and Volvo Penta the rest), and comparable companies are the Atlas Copco/Sandvik type rather than carmakers. Cyclical = yes, because demand for trucks and construction equipment follows the freight and replacement cycle (revenue has fallen from SEK 552bn to 472bn since 2023). stockanalysis' net debt of SEK 213bn and EV multiples include Volvo Financial Services' loan book; the industrial business has net cash.
