    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −25 of −30
    • Margin
      Revenue was only EUR 0.55m in H1 2026 and the operating result −4.6m. A loss every year since the IPO (net result −3.4 to −10.9m EUR in 2021–2025). The small loss in 2025 (−2.4m operating) was due to grants of EUR 16.6m incl. a catch-up adjustment for earlier years.
      bourse.lavenir.net
      −10
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue fell from EUR 11.1m in 2024 to 1.0m in 2025 and consists of consulting and construction support on individual projects. The target of EUR 100m in revenue in 2027 has been abandoned.
      abcbourse.com
      −6
    • Capital allocation
      The 2021 listing raised EUR 106m, and cash is now 27.2m. The project portfolio has been cut from USD 3.0bn to 2.3bn, the Los Cabos project (Mexico) has been scaled down to a third with a loss of EUR 0.5m, and the South Africa project has been written down by EUR 1.5m.
      bourse.lavenir.net
      −5
    • Moat / segment of the future
      The Renewstable concept (solar plus hydrogen storage delivering stable power) is not proven in operation; the first plant (CEOG) is not yet delivering power. The MW fuel cell factory in Blanquefort has only reached the prototype stage (30/6/2026) and must compete with Ballard and other established producers.
      finanzwire.com
      −4

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −24 of −35
    • Execution – CEOG
      The flagship CEOG in French Guiana is delayed by about two years due to conflict with the local population. Start-up was promised for mid-2026, but the first deliveries to the grid are now expected only toward the end of 2026.
      finanzwire.com
      −7
    • Project financing and future dilution
      The portfolio is valued at over 2bn EUR in investments, but each project requires external project financing and equity from HDF. Renewstable Barbados has a licence, but no funding or investment decision has been announced. With cash of 27m EUR, HDF cannot fund its share of several projects without share issues or divestments.
      finanzwire.com
      −6
    • Grant dependence
      The fuel cell programme rests on IPCEI grants from Bpifrance (up to EUR 168.9m, paid out against documented expenses; 25.4m received). The result depends on when the grants are recognised as income, and there is political risk that France cuts hydrogen support.
      bourse.lavenir.net
      −4
    • Control and liquidity
      Founder and CEO Damien Havard owns 48.25% of the capital and 53.7% of the votes, and the free float is about 3m shares. Trading is very thin (about DKK 22,000 a day on Nordnet), so minority shareholders have little influence and find it hard to exit.
      echanges.dila.gouv.fr
      −4
    • Market/sector risk
      The hydrogen sector is under pressure: Lhyfe has an EV of EUR 124m with 121m in debt and large losses, and projects are being scaled down (HDF's own Mexico project to a third). The stock has fallen 49% in a year and 35% in three months.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3
