    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −19 of −30
    • Margin
      EBIT was −CAD 3.0m TTM (−7.7%) and net income −4.9m. Adjusted EBITDA is positive for the fourth quarter in a row (CAD 1.4m in Q2, 12.8% margin), but there have been losses every year in 2021–2025.
      stockanalysis.com
      −7
    • Capital allocation
      The company listed in 2021 at about NOK 30 and has lost money every year since. The 2021 acquisition cost CAD 23.5m. In December 2025 came a rights issue of NOK 300m at NOK 3.00 (+78% shares), underwritten by the main owner, who also converted a shareholder loan.
      filingreader.com
      −5
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue went from CAD 40.9m (2023) to 37.2 (2024) and 35.6 (2025) before rising to 38.8 TTM (+19% in H1 2026). Over five years, growth is about 9% a year from 26.8m (2021), and order intake is uneven and marked by single large system orders.
      stockanalysis.com
      −4
    • Moat
      Tekna has decades of expertise in induction plasma and a strong position in spherical powder (e.g. titanium) for aircraft and defence. Competition is fierce from Colibrium Additive (GE/AP&C), Carpenter Additive, 6K Additive and Oerlikon, and customers can switch powder supplier.
      metal-am.com
      −3

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 3 findings    −8 of −35
    • Controlling shareholder and liquidity
      Arendals Fossekompani owns 72.4%, and free float is approx. 61m shares. Only approx. 52,000 shares trade per day (approx. 0.3m NOK). The minority has no influence, and an exit for the main owner or a squeeze-out at a low price is a risk.
      filingreader.com
      −3
    • Order concentration
      The record order book of CAD 28.5m is lifted by a single systems order of CAD 11.5m (about 40%) from a new US customer in critical minerals. Without it, order intake was about 7.5m, below revenue.
