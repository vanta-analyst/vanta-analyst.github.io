    Bull case

    A binding agreement is signed for a commercial fumed silica plant financed by a major producer, and Novacium's defence prototypes become series orders, so Novacium raises capital worth far more than EUR 30m. If HPQ's 36.8% of Novacium became worth C$50–70m and 50% of HSPI C$50–100m, the market cap could go from 64 to C$150–250m (2–4x) – but only if HPQ avoids heavy dilution at the same time.

    Bear case

    No binding agreements before the cash runs out at year-end, so HPQ must do an issue at C$0.08–0.12 (+20–30% shares) or sell some of its stakes. Novacium and HSPI require more capital, which HPQ cannot follow, so the stakes are diluted. The share price could halve again, as it has fallen 25% in a year and 58% from the market cap in 2021.
