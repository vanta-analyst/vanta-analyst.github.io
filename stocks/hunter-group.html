    Business

    Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? · 4 findings    −24 of −30
    • Quality of growth
      Revenue is USD 117m TTM vs 8.8m in 2025, solely because the TD3C rate has risen from about USD 453,000/day in Q2 to 1.22m/day 2/10, while Hunter pays a fixed charter rate of 51,750. It is the freight cycle and geopolitics (Hormuz, Saudi export halt), not a stronger company.
      theedgemalaysia.com
      −8
    • Moat
      Hunter owns no ships: two VLCCs are chartered in at a fixed rate and chartered out again on TD3C-indexed contracts, and the company has 3 employees. There is no moat.
      inderes.fi
      −7
    • Future segment
      The three-year contracts from Dec 2023 and Mar 2024 expire, and both ships are redelivered within about 6 months. There is no new fleet, and new tonnage now costs record prices (a 15-year-old VLCC has risen from USD 83.5m to 135m in two months), so reinvesting at the top is risky.
      gcaptain.com
      −6
    • Capital allocation
      The share count has risen 14% over a year after heavy dilution in 2024, and the company previously sold its fleet (2021) and changed strategy several times. The 2026 dividends pull in the right direction.
      stockanalysis.com
      −3

    Cracks

    Cash flow, customers, debt, insiders, guidance and what sits behind the headline numbers. · 5 findings    −28 of −35
    • Customer behaviour
      One counterparty has underpaid six months in a row. The claim was USD 55m after August (11.75 of 31.7m unpaid) and grows with the instalments, so it could reach over USD 100m at expiry – about half of the market cap. The case is in arbitration in London (LMAA). The counterparty has an argument: the TD3C route via Hormuz has been commercially unavailable since February, and Baltic calculates the index from Yanbu cargoes plus a risk premium.
      inderes.fi
      −12
    • Customer concentration
      There are only two counterparties (security depots at Mercuria and Trafigura according to the Q1 report), and one of them is behind the dispute. Correction: the old list wrote that both ships are chartered out to the same counterparty, but the announcements speak of 'one of' the counterparties.
      mb.cision.com
      −3

      TD3C was USD 1.22m/day on 2/10 vs normally 30,000–60,000, driven by the Hormuz closure and Saudi export halts. 183 VLCCs were ordered in H1 2026, and a normalisation could make rates fall sharply within days (as in December 2025: −20% in one day).
