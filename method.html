The Vanta method · model 1.2

Every stock starts at 100. Then it has to earn it.

Vanta is a critical scoring framework. Instead of hunting for reasons to like a stock, it looks for reasons to take points away, and only gives points back for strengths that are documented. 210 stocks are scored so far. No stock has reached 100.

Three steps, each with a ceiling

Every deduction is tied to a finding and a source, shown on each company's page. One event is only deducted once. A criterion without data gives 0, so the score is a ceiling, not a floor.

1 · Business max −30

Is the company getting stronger, not just bigger? Margins, return on invested capital, the segment that has to carry the future, the moat, the quality of growth and how management allocates capital.

2 · Price max −35

Today's multiples against the company's own 5–10-year history and its peers. A great business can still be a bad price.

3 · Cracks max −35

What sits behind the headline numbers: cash flow, customer concentration and behaviour, execution, governance, debt, insider trading, guidance and macro exposure.

Plus max +15

Only documented strengths, such as insider buying, raised guidance, net cash or a widely spread customer base.

Three data-driven adjustments

Since model 1.2 three adjustments are added from market data that is refreshed every week. The final score is held between 1 and 100.

Score = 100 − Business − Price − Cracks + Plus + PEG points + Upside points + Revision points

PEG points (−12 to +8)

PEG is the forward P/E divided by expected earnings growth. It asks: how much are you paying for the growth?

Guards: positive PEG points are removed for loss-making companies, when the trailing P/E is more than twice the forward P/E (earnings expected to bounce), and for cyclical companies at a peak.

Upside points (−10 to +10)

Upside is the median analyst price target divided by the price, minus 1. The median is used instead of the average, so one extreme target cannot move it.

Analyst targets are historically too optimistic, so positive upside points are cut back:

  • halved when fewer than 10 analysts cover the stock
  • halved again when the targets are widely spread (high minus low is more than the median)
  • capped at +4 when the stock has fallen 25% or more in a year, because targets tend to lag a fall
  • set to 0 when the price is more than 30 days old.

Revision points (−6 to +6)

How analysts' earnings-per-share estimate for the next year has moved over the last 4–13 weeks. Rising estimates add points; falling estimates take them away. Vanta keeps its own log of estimates, so revision points appear once a stock has at least four weeks of history.

How to read a score

  • 70+ with more than 20% upside is what we call the 70+ zone: a strong business at a price analysts still see room above.
  • 50–70 usually means a good business with a real question mark: the price, a crack, or both.
  • Below 50 means the model found several serious problems. It does not mean the stock will fall.

A score is a starting point for your own research, not a buy or sell signal. The model is evaluated every month against how the stocks actually performed, and changes are logged as new versions.

Data and updates

Prices, analyst targets, PEG, P/E and 52-week changes come from stockanalysis.com and are refreshed every Saturday. Findings are researched per company and link to their sources. Each company page shows the date of its price.

Not investment advice. Vanta is an analysis framework that scores companies; it does not tell anyone what to buy or sell.
